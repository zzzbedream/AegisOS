Verifiable provenance for agentic purchases · x402 on Stellar

A perfect payment is not enough.

AI agents already pay for data with x402. The payment proves the money moved, not what the agent received. If the seller delivers poisoned content, that content becomes the context for the next decision, and the next payment.

AegisOS cuts that chain: it proves each purchase was paid under a signed commitment, counted exactly once, and that the content came from the seller. If the content is tainted, the agent may have paid, but it cannot let that content drive its next action.

01 — The problem

x402 did its job. The content did not.

When paying costs a fraction of a cent, an agent buys thousands of times, and each purchase feeds the next. A hostile seller doesn't need to steal a key: selling poisoned data is enough.

02 — What AegisOS guarantees

Four things a third party can check, without taking the buyer's word.

No paid content may ground a later spend unless there is a prior commitment, a verifiable payment–delivery binding and a deterministic risk assessment.

C

The payment is real and was authorized

A compromised agent cannot pay outside a commitment signed by the owner's authority.

The wallet is a Soroban smart account: its __check_auth requires that commitment and notarizes every payment (sequence number and hash chain) in the same transaction.
A+B

Every payment is counted once, none is left out

The tally of good and bad purchases is not the buyer's word.

The registry recomputes on-chain the account's payment chain up to its head and counts the verdicts itself. Omitting, inventing, editing or reordering a payment breaks the chain.
D

The content came from the seller

Nobody can claim "the seller sent me this" without proof.

The paid request goes through a Reclaim attestor, which signs the HTTP response it saw over TLS, bound to that purchase's commitment.
✂

The cascade is cut

What was bought never becomes authority on its own.

TAINTED content is quarantined, cannot back spend drafts, and the next payment is denied until the owner reviews it.

06 — Why Stellar, and what it costs

Built for micropayments at machine speed.

Stellar makes per-request payments viable; that is exactly what lets poisoned content spread fast. Verification has to cost less than the problem.

0.104
XLM to anchor a real 2-payment range on testnet. One range counts up to 64 payments in a single transaction.
43 M
instructions for the largest range (64 + a 192-payment tail), out of 400 M allowed per transaction.
15/15
attacks blocked in the covered families (95% lower bound: 81.9%). 0/5 false positives.
200+
automated TypeScript tests, plus 47 for the Soroban contracts.

Stated limits

  • Guarantee D only applies to public sellers: the attestor has to reach the seller's URL.
  • No layer proves the content is true. D proves the seller sent it; plausible but false data needs oracles or arbitration.
  • Injection detection uses known patterns and publishes its misses: 5 of 20 corpus attacks get through (encoded payloads, homoglyphs, multi-turn attacks).
  • The receipt's verdict is the buyer's attestation, not proof against the seller.
  • Everything runs on Stellar testnet.
AegisOS

x402 answers whether the payment happened. AegisOS answers whether what was received may enter memory, back a decision or unlock a later spend.
